# Addendum: Making the Projective Motion Blur Model Fit a Drone

Sections 13–20, continuing `MATH_FOUNDATION.md`. The question addressed here is
not *where does the model break* (§2, §11 already answer that) but **what can be
added to the mathematics so that the model holds onto a drone's requirements.**

The organising observation is that §3–§10 never use the fact that $\mathcal{H}_t$
is a homography. They use only

$$
\mathbf{B} = \sum_j w_j \mathbf{T}_j,
\qquad \mathbf{T}_j \text{ a differentiable sampler, linear in } \mathbf{u}
$$

So every additive extension below is a change to **§2 alone**. The adjoint (§5),
the sensor model (§6), the ADMM scheme (§7.3) and the whiteness test (§10) carry
over untouched. That modularity is the model's real asset.

---

## 13. The trajectory as a curve in $\mathfrak{se}(3)$, not a multiset of poses

### 13.1 Twist parameterisation

Replace "$T$ free poses" with "one rigid-body flow sampled $T$ times". Over a
$10$ ms exposure the pose is a second-order Taylor expansion on the group:

$$
g(t) \;=\; g_{c}\,\exp\!\Bigl(\hat\xi\,(t-t_c) + \tfrac12\,\hat{\dot\xi}\,(t-t_c)^2\Bigr),
\qquad
\xi = \begin{bmatrix}\nu\\ \omega\end{bmatrix}\in\mathfrak{se}(3)
\tag{22}
$$

Parameter count collapses:

| Model | Free parameters ($T=25$) |
|---|---|
| §2.2 rotation-only, free poses | $3T = 75$ |
| naive 6-DOF, free poses | $6T = 150$ |
| **(22), constant twist** | $\mathbf{6}$ |
| **(22), with $\dot\xi$** | $\mathbf{12}$ |

This is not merely economy. It changes the *identifiability class* (§14) and
turns estimation into linear algebra (§16).

### 13.2 The blur field is the image of one twist under the interaction matrix

Differentiate the projection $u = fX/Z$, $v=fY/Z$ along the flow
$\dot X = -\nu - \omega\times X$. With $X/Z = u/f$, $Y/Z = v/f$:

$$
\boxed{\;
\frac{d}{dt}\begin{bmatrix}u\\v\end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{L}(u,v,Z)\,\xi,
\quad
\mathbf{L}=
\begin{bmatrix}
-\frac{f}{Z} & 0 & \frac{u}{Z} & \frac{uv}{f} & -\bigl(f+\frac{u^2}{f}\bigr) & v\\[4pt]
0 & -\frac{f}{Z} & \frac{v}{Z} & f+\frac{v^2}{f} & -\frac{uv}{f} & -u
\end{bmatrix}\;}
\tag{23}
$$

$\mathbf{L}$ is the classical **image interaction matrix** of visual servoing.
Hence, to first order in $\tau$,

$$
\Delta^{(t)}(\mathbf{i}) \;=\; (t-t_c)\,\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{i})\,\xi
\;+\;\tfrac12 (t-t_c)^2\Bigl[\mathbf{L}\dot\xi + \bigl(D_\mathbf{i}\mathbf{L}\cdot\mathbf{L}\xi + \partial_Z\mathbf{L}\cdot\dot Z\bigr)\xi\Bigr] + O(\tau^3)
\tag{24}
$$

Two consequences worth stating plainly:

1. **The local PSF at every pixel is a straight segment** of direction
   $\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{i})\xi/\|\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{i})\xi\|$ and length
   $\tau\|\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{i})\xi\|$, to first order. The entire
   spatially-varying kernel field — $N$ kernels — is generated by **6 numbers
   through a known algebraic map**. §4 said $3T$ numbers generate $N$ kernels;
   (23) sharpens that to $6$, and makes the map explicit rather than implicit.
2. **The second-order term is the kernel's curvature.** Straight-vs-curved local
   PSF is therefore a direct measurement of $\dot\xi$, i.e. of angular/linear
   acceleration — which a drone's IMU also reports, giving a consistency check
   that the rotation-only model has no analogue for.

> *Suggested implementation:* `se3.py :: interaction_matrix`, `twist_offsets`.

## 14. Ordering: from $T!$ down to one bit

§3.1 is the most quoted obstruction — the trajectory is recoverable only as a
multiset. Under (22) it weakens dramatically.

> **Proposition.** Let $\{\theta^{(t_n)}\}_{n<T}$ be a uniform-in-$t$ sample of an
> injective $C^2$ arc, sampled finely relative to the arc's reach (local feature
> size). Then the multiset determines the ordered sequence **up to reversal** —
> exactly two admissible orderings.

*Proof sketch.* For points lying on a simple arc, sampled below its reach, the
minimum-length Hamiltonian path through them is the arc order; the reverse
attains the same length, and no other ordering does. Uniform-in-$t$ sampling then
fixes the parameterisation up to the affine map $t\mapsto \tau-t$. $\square$

*Numerical check.* Over random cubic arcs with $T=7$, exhaustive enumeration of
all $5040$ orderings scored by total squared second difference: **exactly 2**
attain the minimum, and they are $(0,\dots,6)$ and $(6,\dots,0)$. Reproduced on
every trial.

The remaining bit is the sign of time, and the gyro resolves it: $\mathrm{sign}
\langle \omega_{\text{IMU}}, \hat\omega\rangle$. So:

$$
\boxed{\;\text{multiset}\;\xrightarrow{\;\text{curve constraint}\;}\;\text{2 orderings}\;\xrightarrow{\;\text{gyro sign}\;}\;\text{ordered 6-DOF path}\;}
$$

**Corollary — §8 becomes unnecessary.** Optimal transport was needed only because
poses were permutation-ambiguous. Under (22) a trajectory is a point
$\xi\in\mathbb{R}^6$ (or $\mathbb{R}^{12}$), and the image-plane cost of eq. (19)
linearises to a plain quadratic form:

$$
d^2(\xi,\xi') \;=\; (\xi-\xi')^\top \mathbf{G}\,(\xi-\xi'),
\qquad
\mathbf{G} \;=\; \frac{\tau^2}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{i}} \mathbf{L}(\mathbf{i})^\top\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{i})
\tag{25}
$$

$\mathbf{G}$ is the Gram matrix of the interaction matrix. §8's argument ("use
image-plane distance, not rotation-space distance") is precisely the statement
that the correct metric tensor on $\mathfrak{se}(3)$ is $\mathbf{G}$, not $\mathbf{I}$.
The EMD is replaced by a $6\times6$ matrix.

## 15. Identifiability as $\operatorname{rank}\mathbf{L}$ — §2.3 made computable

Stack (23) over pixels: $\mathbb{L}\in\mathbb{R}^{2N\times6}$,
$\mathbb{L} = \tau\,[\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{i}_1);\ \mathbf{L}(\mathbf{i}_2);\ \dots]$, so
that $\mathbb{L}\xi$ is the blur displacement field in pixels. Then

> $\xi$ is identifiable from the blur field $\iff \operatorname{rank}\mathbb{L}=6$,
> and $\operatorname{null}(\mathbb{L})$ **is** the rotation–translation ambiguity of eq. (4).

Compare the $\nu_x$ column, $(-f/Z,\,0)$, with the $\omega_y$ column,
$(-(f+u^2/f),\,-uv/f)$. They are collinear up to $O(u^2/f^2)$. Therefore the
degeneracy is lifted by exactly two things: **depth variation** ($1/Z$ spread) and
**field of view** ($u^2/f^2$).

### 15.1 The degenerate limit is exactly 2-dimensional

Singular values of $\mathbb{L}$ at constant depth $Z=5$ m, $f=458$, shrinking the
image to half-FOV $\pm p$ px:

| half-FOV $p$ | $\sigma_5$ | $\sigma_6$ | $\sigma_6/\sigma_1$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $800$ px | $1.046$ | $1.046$ | $4.5\times10^{-2}$ |
| $200$ px | $0.1528$ | $0.1528$ | $1.5\times10^{-2}$ |
| $50$ px | $0.01016$ | $0.01016$ | $1.1\times10^{-3}$ |
| $10$ px | $4.1\times10^{-4}$ | $4.1\times10^{-4}$ | $4.4\times10^{-5}$ |
| $2$ px | $2\times10^{-5}$ | $2\times10^{-5}$ | $1.8\times10^{-6}$ |

$\sigma_5=\sigma_6$ to machine precision at every FOV — the null space is a clean
2-plane, confirming eq. (4) has exactly two absorbed directions
$(\nu_x,\omega_y)$ and $(\nu_y,\omega_x)$, and no more.

### 15.2 Wide FOV is the cure, not the disease

§2.2(b) says the rotation-only reduction *fails* at short $f$. The dual statement
is that short $f$ is what makes 6-DOF *recoverable*. Conditioning of $\mathbb{L}$
over a $752\times480$ frame, nominal motion $2$ m/s and $2$ rad/s, $\tau=10$ ms:

| Configuration | $\operatorname{cond}\mathbb{L}$ | $\sigma_6$ (px) | weakest direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| $f=1280$, planar $Z=5$ m | $244$ | $0.110$ | $0.98\,\nu_y + 0.19\,\omega_x$ |
| $f=458$ (EuRoC), planar $Z=5$ m | $41.5$ | $0.282$ | $0.98\,\nu_y + 0.18\,\omega_x$ |
| $f=250$, planar $Z=5$ m | $25.0$ | $0.399$ | $0.99\,\nu_y + 0.13\,\omega_x$ |
| $f=458$, depth $2$–$20$ m | $19.0$ | $0.613$ | $0.72\,\nu_y - 0.69\,\nu_z$ |
| $f=250$, depth $2$–$20$ m | $22.1$ | $0.451$ | $0.95\,\nu_y - 0.31\,\nu_z$ |

Two readings. First, FOV buys an order of magnitude ($244\to25$) — a drone's wide
lens is a **structural advantage** for trajectory recovery, exactly inverting the
usual complaint. Second, once depth varies the weakest direction stops being the
classical $(\nu_y,\omega_x)$ pair and becomes $(\nu_y,\nu_z)$: rotation is fully
separated, and what is left is the ordinary lateral-vs-axial translation
weakness.

### 15.3 $\sigma_6(\mathbb{L})$ as a per-frame admissibility test

$\sigma_6$ is computable from intrinsics and a depth map alone — no image, no
solve. The dimensionally correct gate is not $\sigma_6$ against a pixel noise
floor but the **achievable precision along the weakest twist direction**, which
for the estimator of §16 is

$$
\delta\xi_{\text{worst}} \;=\; \frac{\sigma_{\text{meas}}}{\sigma_{\min}(\mathbb{L})}
\;\;\lesssim\;\; \text{tolerance}
\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; \text{6-DOF recovery is well-posed on this frame}
\tag{27a}
$$

with $\mathbb{L}$ *unnormalised*, so that $\sigma_{\min}\propto\sqrt{n}$ and using
more pixels genuinely helps. Report $\sigma_6/\sqrt{n}$ separately as the
scale-free, image-size-independent number. Measured on a $192\times256$ frame at
stride 8 ($n=768$), $\sigma_{\text{meas}}=0.5$ px: planar $Z=5$ m gives
$\delta\xi_{\text{worst}}=0.87$ (98% translational) — unusable; a $2$–$10$ m depth
ramp gives $0.108$; with the gyro fixed (§17) the translational precision
reaches $0.016$–$0.019$ m/s laterally, though $\nu_z$ stays at $0.09$ m/s.

This is the missing gate. §10's whiteness test tells you *after the fact* that the
model was inadequate; $\sigma_6$ tells you *before* whether the frame carries the
information at all. Log it alongside $\rho_1$.

> *Suggested implementation:* `identify.py :: interaction_stack`, `admissibility`.

## 16. Closed-form 6-DOF estimation — no network required for the leading term

Because the local PSF is a straight segment (§13.2), its direction and length are
directly measurable per patch (cepstrum, spectral-zero lines, or autocorrelation
of $\nabla v$). Collect measurements $\hat{\mathbf d}\in\mathbb{R}^{2N}$; then

$$
\hat\xi \;=\; \arg\min_\xi \|\mathbb{L}\xi - \hat{\mathbf d}\|^2 \;=\; (\mathbb{L}^\top\mathbb{L})^{-1}\mathbb{L}^\top\hat{\mathbf d},
\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(\hat\xi) = \sigma_{\text{meas}}^2\,(\mathbb{L}^\top\mathbb{L})^{-1}
\tag{26}
$$

A **linear least squares problem in 6 unknowns.** Monte-Carlo validation at
$\sigma_{\text{meas}}=0.5$ px, $200$ trials, true
$\xi=(1.5,-0.8,2.0\ \mathrm{m/s};\ 0.6,-1.2,0.4\ \mathrm{rad/s})$:

| Config | $\sigma(\nu_x,\nu_y,\nu_z)$ m/s | $\sigma(\omega_x,\omega_y,\omega_z)$ rad/s | predicted by (26) |
|---|---|---|---|
| $f=458$, planar $Z=5$ | $0.102,\ 0.133,\ 0.038$ | $0.024,\ 0.016,\ 0.008$ | $0.104,\ 0.136,\ 0.037$ / $0.024,\ 0.016,\ 0.008$ |
| $f=458$, depth $2$–$20$ | $0.042,\ 0.049,\ 0.050$ | $0.007,\ 0.006,\ 0.009$ | matches to 3% |
| $f=1280$, planar $Z=5$ | $0.234,\ 0.349,\ 0.033$ | $0.069,\ 0.046,\ 0.008$ | matches to 3% |

Empirical and predicted standard deviations agree to within 3% everywhere, so
(26) is not a heuristic — the covariance is exact and reportable.

**Why this matters for §7.1.** The trivial degeneracy ($\theta\equiv0$,
$\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{v}$, loss exactly zero) is a property of *joint* gradient
descent on $(\mathbf{u},\theta)$. Equation (26) is a non-iterative estimate of
$\xi$ that never touches $\mathbf{u}$, so it cannot fall into that basin. Used as
initialisation, it supplies exactly the explicit prior §7.1 says a non-learning
pipeline must have.

## 17. The IMU as a *constraint*, not a prior — closing §7.1 properly

A drone measures $\omega(t)$ directly. Under (22) that fixes three of the six
components of $\xi$ outright, leaving

$$
\hat\nu \;=\; \arg\min_\nu \bigl\|\mathbb{L}_{\nu}\,\nu - (\hat{\mathbf d} - \mathbb{L}_\omega\,\omega_{\text{IMU}})\bigr\|^2
\tag{27}
$$

a **3-unknown** problem. Measured effect:

| Config | $\operatorname{cond}$ | $\sigma(\nu)$ m/s |
|---|---|---|
| free 6-DOF, $f=458$, planar $Z=5$ | $40.7$ | $0.102,\ 0.133,\ 0.037$ |
| **gyro-constrained**, same frame | $\mathbf{1.7}$ | $0.023,\ 0.025,\ 0.037$ |
| **gyro-constrained**, depth $2$–$20$ | $2.2$ | $0.026,\ 0.029,\ 0.048$ |

Conditioning improves by $24\times$ and the planar-scene degeneracy of §15.1
disappears entirely — because the ambiguity of eq. (4) is between $\nu$ and
$\omega$, and $\omega$ is no longer free.

The structural point is stronger than the numbers:

> $\theta\equiv0$ is **not in the feasible set** when $\omega$ is measured.
> §7.1's global optimum at zero blur is *excluded by constraint*, not escaped by
> a deep-image-prior argument.

That is the single largest thing a drone adds that hand-held shake cannot.

### 17.1 IMU error budget over one exposure

Double integration is harmless at $\tau=10$ ms; the risks are elsewhere.

| Source | Magnitude | Image-plane effect ($f=458$, $Z=2$ m) |
|---|---|---|
| accel bias $0.01$ m/s² | $5\times10^{-7}$ m | $\sim10^{-4}$ px — negligible |
| $1°$ gravity misalignment | $8.5\times10^{-6}$ m | $0.002$ px — negligible |
| velocity error $50$ mm/s (from VIO) | $5\times10^{-4}$ m | $0.11$ px |
| **IMU–shutter time offset $1$ ms** ($\omega=2$ rad/s) | — | $\mathbf{0.9\ px}$ |
| exposure $\tau$ error $10\%$ | — | $0.9$ px |

Time synchronisation and $\tau$ consume the entire budget; inertial integration
consumes none. §9 already gives the tool for $\tau$ — extend its whiteness sweep
to a 2-D sweep over $(\tau, t_{\text{offset}})$ and both are measured, not assumed.

## 18. The parallax/depth duality

This is the cleanest addition, and it removes the sense that §11 is a dead end.

> **Proposition.** The term that invalidates the rotation-only homography and the
> term that encodes scene depth in the blur are **the same term**, with the same
> magnitude $f v \tau / Z$.

Because $\delta_{\text{parallax}} = fv\tau\bigl(\tfrac1{Z_{\min}}-\tfrac1{Z_{\max}}\bigr)$
and $\delta_{\text{trans}} = fv\tau/Z$ are the same expression (eq. 21). Hence the
regime where the model breaks is exactly the regime where the missing information
is above the noise floor. Define the crossover depth

$$
Z^\star \;=\; \frac{f\,v\,\tau}{\delta_{\text{tol}}}
\;=\;\frac{458\times2\times0.01}{1\ \mathrm{px}} \;=\; 9.16\ \mathrm{m}
\tag{28}
$$

| $Z$ (m) | $fv\tau/Z$ (px) | rotation-only | $\delta Z/Z$ from blur at $\pm0.5$ px |
|---|---|---|---|
| $1$ | $9.16$ | fails | $5\%$ |
| $2$ | $4.58$ | fails | $11\%$ |
| $3$ | $3.05$ | fails | $16\%$ |
| $5$ | $1.83$ | fails | $27\%$ |
| $\mathbf{9.16}$ | $\mathbf{1.00}$ | **boundary** | $50\%$ |
| $15$ | $0.61$ | valid | $82\%$ |
| $30$ | $0.31$ | valid | $164\%$ |

$$
\boxed{\;Z<Z^\star:\ \text{model invalid, but depth is measurable from the blur.}\quad
Z>Z^\star:\ \text{depth unmeasurable, but the model is valid anyway.}\;}
$$

There is no regime where you are both wrong and blind. With $\xi$ known from
§17, inverting (23) pixelwise gives inverse depth in closed form:

$$
\frac{1}{Z(\mathbf{i})}
=\frac{\bigl\langle \Delta(\mathbf{i})/\tau - \mathbf{L}_\omega(\mathbf{i})\,\omega,\;\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{i})\nu\bigr\rangle}{\|\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{i})\nu\|^2},
\qquad
\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{i})=\begin{bmatrix}-f&0&u\\0&-f&v\end{bmatrix}
\tag{29}
$$

So **depth stops being a required external input** (§2.3's "any 6-DOF experiment
must supply $d$ externally") and becomes an output, wherever it is needed at all.
Stereo remains the better estimate; (29) is the fallback and the cross-check.

## 19. Rolling shutter is a one-parameter left action

Rolling shutter is listed in §1 as assumption 2 and in §10 as an undetectable-proof
violation ($\rho_1$ never below $0.884$). Under (22) it costs **one scalar**.

Row $r$ integrates over $[t_0+\alpha r,\ t_0+\alpha r+\tau]$. Since
$g(t)=g_c\exp(\hat\xi (t-t_c))$, shifting the row's exposure centre is a left
multiplication:

$$
g_r(t) \;=\; \exp\!\bigl(\alpha r\,\hat\xi\bigr)\,g(t)
\tag{30}
$$

The operator structure of §3–§5 is unchanged; only the per-pixel offset lookup
gains a row-dependent time origin, which §4's offset field already supports since
offsets are per-pixel by construction. So rolling shutter is **not a model-class
extension** — it is a reparameterisation with one calibratable constant $\alpha$
(line time), fixed once per camera.

Combined with §17, the estimation problem for a rolling-shutter drone camera is
$(\nu,\ \tau,\ t_{\text{offset}})$ — five scalars, with $\alpha$ and $\omega$
known. Small enough for exhaustive global search over the whiteness objective,
which removes the last reason to fear §7.1's local minima.

## 20. Multi-frame: an $SE(3)$ spline, and a provable kill of the trivial optimum

A drone flies continuously, so consecutive frames sample one curve. Fit a single
cubic $B$-spline $g(t)$ on $SE(3)$ with control points $\{c_m\}$ spaced at, say,
$50$ ms; frame $k$'s blur operator is determined by the segment
$[t_k,\ t_k+\tau]$. Unknowns per frame drop below six, and — unlike §14's
two-fold reversal ambiguity — the ordering is now globally determined by
continuity across frames. §3.1 is fully resolved, not merely reduced.

Add the cross-frame consistency term to (15):

$$
\min_{\{\mathbf{u}_k\},\,\{c_m\},\,Z}\;
\sum_k \tfrac12\bigl\|R(\mathbf{B}_k\mathbf{u}_k)-\mathbf{v}_k\bigr\|^2
+\lambda\sum_k\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{u}_k)
+\mu\sum_k\bigl\|\mathcal{W}_{k\to k+1}(\mathbf{u}_k)-\mathbf{u}_{k+1}\bigr\|^2
\tag{31}
$$

with $\mathcal{W}$ the depth-aware inter-frame warp. At the trivial point
$\mathbf{u}_k=\mathbf{v}_k$, $\xi=0$, the first two terms vanish but the third does
**not**: consecutive blurry frames are not related by a sharp rigid warp, since
each carries a different blur. Therefore

> The zero-blur explanation is no longer a global optimum of (31) for any $\mu>0$.

§7.1's degeneracy is removed by a term that costs nothing to write and is
justified by the drone's own kinematics.

## 21. Revised approximation budget

Superseding the table in §11 for the 6-DOF case.

| Approximation | Cost | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| $\mathbf{B}^\top$ by autograd | $1.7\times10^{-16}$ | none — exact, survives §13–§20 |
| twist model (22), constant $\xi$ | $f\tau^2\|\dot\omega\|/8$ | $0.06$ px at $\dot\omega=10$ rad/s²; $0.29$ px at $50$; $0.57$ px at $100$ |
| twist model with $\dot\xi$ | $O(\tau^3)$ | negligible |
| first-order $\Delta = \tau\mathbf{L}\xi$ | kernel curvature ignored | valid when $\tau\|\mathbf{L}\dot\xi\|\ll\tau\|\mathbf{L}\xi\|$ |
| rolling shutter via (30) | exact | $\alpha$ calibrated |
| rotation-only | unbounded | **only** if $Z_{\min}>Z^\star$ (28) |
| depth from (29) | $\delta Z/Z = \sigma_{\text{meas}}Z/(fv\tau)$ | $Z<Z^\star$ |

New failure modes introduced, which the original document does not cover:

- **Occlusion.** A depth-aware warp is a scatter with $z$-buffering, not an
  invertible gather. Linearity in $\mathbf{u}$ survives (visibility depends on $Z$,
  not on $\mathbf{u}$), so §5.3's autograd adjoint is still exact — but gradients
  are discontinuous at depth boundaries and holes must be renormalised. This is
  the one place the extension gets no cover from existing theory.
- **$\nu_z$ conditioning.** Forward flight — the dominant drone mode — produces
  radial displacement $r\,v_z\tau/Z$ with **no $f$ amplification**: $\approx1.2$ px
  at $r=300$ px, $Z=5$ m, versus $\approx9$ px for yaw. The most-used DOF is the
  least-encoded one, and it is confusable with defocus. §15's tables show it
  becoming the weak direction as soon as rotation is separated.
- **Lens distortion.** Eq. (23) is pinhole. Wide drone optics require
  $\mathcal{D}\circ\exp(\hat\xi t)\circ\mathcal{D}^{-1}$, or undistortion first.

## 23. Exposure time is unobservable from a single image — at every order

§9 states that $\tau$ is identifiable *given measured $\omega(t)$*. The converse
deserves to be stated as sharply, because it decides how any
"trajectory $\times$ exposure" model must be built.

> **Proposition (time-scale degeneracy).** Let $\theta:[-\tau/2,\tau/2]\to SE(3)$
> be the exposure trajectory, sampled uniformly at $T$ instants, and let
> $\alpha>0$. Define $\tilde\tau = \alpha\tau$ and $\tilde\theta(s)=\theta(s/\alpha)$.
> Then $\mathbf{B}_{\tilde\theta,\tilde\tau} = \mathbf{B}_{\theta,\tau}$ **exactly**,
> and the blurry image is bit-identical.

*Proof.* Uniform sampling of $\tilde\theta$ on $[-\alpha\tau/2,\alpha\tau/2]$
yields the same *set* of poses $\{\theta(t_n)\}$ with the same weights $w_j$. By
§3.1, $\mathbf{B}$ depends on the poses only as a weighted multiset. $\square$

In the twist parameterisation (22) the degeneracy is the jet rescaling

$$
\tau\mapsto\alpha\tau,\quad
\xi\mapsto\xi/\alpha,\quad
\dot\xi\mapsto\dot\xi/\alpha^2,\quad
\ddot\xi\mapsto\ddot\xi/\alpha^3,\ \dots
\tag{32}
$$

so $\tau$ appears only inside $\tau\xi$, $\tau^2\dot\xi$, … Intra-exposure
acceleration does **not** break it — a natural first guess, and wrong.

*Numerical verification.* Rendering at $(\tau,\xi)$ versus $(\alpha\tau,\xi/\alpha)$
for $\alpha\in\{0.25,0.5,2,4\}$ gives RMS difference $0.000$ grey levels
(bit-identical). Repeating with $\dot\omega$ up to $3000$ rad/s² under the
consistent rescaling (32) is likewise bit-identical; only the *naive*
$\dot\xi\mapsto\dot\xi/\alpha$ differs, by $0.66$–$6.7$ grey levels — which is
the signature of a mis-specified model, not of observability.

**Consequences.**

1. $\tau$ must come from outside the image: a rate measurement (gyro), or a
   second frame. There is no third option.
2. §16's estimator returns $\tau\xi$, not $\xi$. Reporting metric velocity from
   a single blurry image *requires* a stated $\tau$; a method that omits this is
   silently assuming one.
3. §9's whiteness sweep over $\tau$ is well-posed **only because** EuRoC supplies
   $\omega(t)$. On a dataset without a gyro the sweep would be flat.

| Route to $\tau$ | Requires | Precision at $f{=}458$, $\sigma_{\text{meas}}{=}0.5$ px |
|---|---|---|
| $\tau=\text{extent}/(f\|\omega_{\text{IMU}}\|)$ | synchronised gyro | $5.5\%$ at $\tau{=}10$ ms, $\omega{=}2$; $3.6\%$ at $\tau{=}5$ ms, $\omega{=}6$ |

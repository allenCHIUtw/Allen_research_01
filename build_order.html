6-DoF Deblur Build Order

End-to-end research plan: EuRoC as the trajectory and geometry source, synthesis for images, exposure-time estimation as the contribution. Every phase has a gate that can stop it.

Settled — do not relitigate
  • Data. EuRoC V1/V2 supplies real drone trajectories, hardware-synced IMU, 6-DoF Vicon pose, and a Leica room scan. It does not supply sharp images, exposure time, or rolling shutter — and those three are exactly what a renderer sets itself.
  • Never deblur your own ground truth. Sharp GT comes from synthesis, never from IMU-deblurring EuRoC frames. See the traps below.
  • Global shutter for paper one. Rolling shutter is a second paper with its own data.
  • Classical before learned. The optimisation pipeline is the baseline, the debugger, and the data generator.
  • The contribution is exposure time. Joint estimation of τ, toffset and the trajectory, with the identifiability argument for why one image alone cannot do it.

Phases

The walkthrough

P0wk 1–3

Foundations and data unpacking

  • Unpack V1_01_easy.zip and verify what the docs don't: the point-cloud path (community convention is mav0/pointcloud0/data.ply, unconfirmed) and the state_groundtruth_estimate0 rate.
  • Build the loader: Vicon pose + 200 Hz IMU + Leica room scan, all in one coordinate convention. Write the frame-convention test first — this is where silent sign errors live.
  • Extend pmbm6: occlusion-aware z-buffered scatter, saturation R(·), sensor noise, and the rolling-shutter path (built now, used in P7).
GateAdjoint test at 10-16. A planted twist recovers within the predicted covariance. Rendered depth from the Leica scan reprojects onto real EuRoC frames with edges aligned.
P1wk 3–6

The synthesiser — your central asset

Everything downstream depends on this, so it gets three weeks rather than a weekend.

  • Sharp latents: frames from V1_01 / V2_01 where motion is slowest. These keep EuRoC's real texture, sensor noise and photometrics, which a COCO image does not have.
  • Trajectories: real Vicon segments of length τ, resampled from the 200 Hz IMU. This is the whole point — real drone dynamics, not hand tremor.
  • Depth: render the Leica scan through the Vicon pose to get a per-frame depth map.
  • Controls: τ, α, noise level, saturation, and a virtual camera orientation — so you can render a nadir view from the same room scan and compare against forward-facing.
GateRound-trip: plant ξ, render, re-estimate. Recovery within the covariance predicted from the Fisher matrix. If it isn't, the renderer is wrong, not the estimator.
P2wk 6–7

Information audit

Compute the weighted Fisher matrix using the sharp image's structure tensor and the depth variance, per envelope, on real EuRoC geometry.

GateEuRoC's camera is forward-facing, so surge sits on the optical axis — the envelope with no rotational proxy and no f amplification. Decide here whether to render nadir instead, or to drop metric surge from your claims. Cheap now, expensive later.
P3wk 7–11

Non-blind deconvolution — the project pivot

Simplest complete pipeline: ξ handed over by the gyro, solve for u only, TV prior, linearised ADMM. Run it three ways — rotation-only, 6-DoF with Leica depth, 6-DoF with a degraded depth map — so depth quality is isolated from model class.

Gate — go / no-go for the whole projectSweep λ and run the whiteness test. Does 6-DoF whiten the residual where rotation-only cannot? If rotation-only already whitens on EuRoC geometry, your premise is weaker than assumed — stop and reconsider before building anything else. Respect the one-sidedness: conclude "inadequate" only if no λ whitens.
P4wk 11–13

Bandlimited SE(3) trajectory

Take the PSD of EuRoC's own 200 Hz gyro — measure fmax, don't guess it — and set the curve degree from K ≳ 2·f_max·τ + 1. Cubic B-spline on SE(3). Decouple the quadrature count T from the parameter count K: use T ≳ blur extent in pixels so PSFs come out as streaks, not dotted lines.

GateThe curve reproduces the Vicon trajectory to sub-pixel in the G = ΣLᵀL metric — not Euclidean on Euler angles.
P5wk 13–19

Exposure time and timing offset — the contribution

  • Two routes: gyro-anchored, τ = extent / (f·|ω|); and the blur-to-flow ratio, τ/Tframe = extent / flow. At EuRoC's 20 fps the inter-frame flow is ~45.8 px against ~9.2 px of blur, so the ratio is well conditioned.
  • The stereo trick: EuRoC's documented flaw is that the two cameras auto-expose independently. Both see the same ω, so the ratio of their blur extents is τ0/τ1, trajectory cancelled. Cross-check it against the brightness ratio — a validation signal that needs no ground truth, available only because of a dataset defect.
  • Then toffset: 2-D whiteness sweep over (τ, toffset). EuRoC is hardware-triggered so this should come out near zero — which makes it a clean sanity check on your estimator.
GateEuRoC cannot validate τ — it has no exposure metadata in any form. Validate on TUM VI (per-frame exposure in ns) and BSD (controlled 1–24 ms), match or beat Korčák & Matas, then apply to EuRoC. If it doesn't hold up on TUM VI, the contribution isn't there.
P6wk 19–27

The learned model

Architecture follows the maths, not convention:

  • Predict curve coefficients (~18–30 numbers), not T free poses. The parameterisation is the inductive bias and this is the highest-leverage choice.
  • Condition on the IMU — feed the gyro segment alongside the image. That breaks the τ degeneracy inside the network instead of hoping it learns something provably absent from the image.
  • Predict the residual from the IMU trajectory, not the absolute. Easier problem, graceful degradation.
  • Supervise with min(d(ξ,ξ′), d(ξ,−ξ′)) under G. Never EMD — it is exactly zero for every permutation, so it cannot teach ordering.
  • Drop the roll / pitch-yaw branch split: roll and νz share the same ∝r radial profile.
GateDoes the learned prior beat BM3D given that the IMU already supplies the trajectory? If not, publish the classical pipeline and say so — that is a finding, not a failure.
P7wk 27–31

Real-data validation, then rolling shutter

Everything to this point is synthetic. Close it out on data you did not generate: Köhler (the only real set with GT camera trajectory), RealBlur, and Gen3-DroneFlight (real drone, sharp GT, mocap, exposure explicitly set to 10/30/50 ms).

Rolling shutter starts here as paper two: simulate for development since you control α, then validate on WHU-RSVI (RS + IMU, readout time published) or BS-RSCD (RS + blur + sharp GT). Calibrate α on hardware by photographing a mains-flicker LED — the banding period gives the line time directly.

GateCheck separately whether the RS shear is being absorbed into u. That failure is invisible to PSNR and fatal for photogrammetry.

Traps

Circular ground truthIf you deblur EuRoC with your model and call the output sharp GT, you are measuring how well your method reproduces itself. Every systematic error — wrong τ, missing νz, unmodelled parallax — becomes permanently undetectable, and a network trained on it learns your errors as physics. IMU-deblurred frames are acceptable as pseudo-labels for pretraining only, with final evaluation on real pairs.
V1_01_easy orientation GTKnown to be wrong. OpenVINS ships a corrected version from their vicon2gt tool. The images are fine — only the rotation ground truth is bad, and rotation is what you're estimating.
Vicon has no device timestampsOfficially acknowledged. Sync between sensor data and motion GT is limited by separate recording systems. Hand-eye and time alignment is a real preprocessing step, not a formality.
Coordinate conventions differGround truth, IMU and camera frames use different axis conventions — evo warns about this explicitly. In interaction-matrix work a sign error is silent and looks like a modelling failure.
Machine Hall is position-onlyLeica laser tracker, 3-DoF, and no room scan. Use V1/V2 for anything trajectory-related. MH sequences also open with the MAV being picked up and set down — skip that segment.
νz conditioningForward flight gives radial displacement with no f amplification: ~1.2 px against ~9 px for yaw. The most-used axis is the least-encoded one, and it is confusable with defocus. Don't promise metric forward velocity.
Soft depth boundariesWhenever you swap the Leica scan for a learned depth map, occlusion boundaries go soft where true depth is discontinuous. With a z-buffered scatter, that is where artefacts appear — and it is the one place existing theory gives no cover.
